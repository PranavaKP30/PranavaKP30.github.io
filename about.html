About me

Hi, I'm Pranav.

I'm an Electrical Engineering student at Georgia Tech in the Circuit Technology and Bioengineering threads. I like building hardware that has to work in the real world: circuits, power, and firmware that hold up outside the lab.

I grew up in Franklin, Massachusetts, and got into engineering by building things for people I cared about. One of my first real projects was a simple inventory system for my grandfather's shop, and it taught me that good engineering starts with understanding the person who will actually use what you build. That idea has stuck with me through every project since.

In high school I spent years in VEX Robotics, where I served as Vice President of my school's robotics team and learned the basics of mechanical design, wiring, and debugging under pressure. I also led a five-person team at NYU Tandon to build CareWatch, a wearable that helps caregivers keep track of loved ones with dementia. That project pulled me toward embedded systems and wearables.

Before starting at Georgia Tech, I joined Northeastern University's Movement Neuroscience Lab, first as a research intern and then as a visiting researcher. There I wrote real-time C++ software that controlled brain stimulation devices and streamed EEG and EMG data during live human experiments. Seeing my code run with real participants made me care a lot about reliability, timing, and safety.

Now, at Georgia Tech's Neuroloops Lab, I'm on the hardware side: designing a modular flex-PCB wearable that records EEG and EMG and delivers safe, isolated stimulation. I handle the schematics, PCB layout, and firmware, and I've learned that the hardest problems usually sit where analog circuits, power, and software meet.

What I bring

  • Hardware design: schematics, 4-layer flex PCB layout, analog front ends, isolated power and stimulation circuits
  • Embedded and real-time software: C++, Python, PlatformIO, ESP32, Xenomai real-time Linux
  • Communication protocols: SPI, I2C, UART, TCP/IP, BLE
  • Lab skills: oscilloscopes, multimeters, power supplies, board bring-up, and hardware validation

Outside of engineering

I'm a top-5% tutor on Schoolhouse.world, where I've taught learners across three continents and mentored other tutors. I'm a big Formula 1 fan, and when I'm not watching races I'm usually go-karting with friends.
