About me

Hi, I'm Pranav.

I'm an Electrical Engineering student at Georgia Tech, in the Circuit Technology and Bioengineering threads. I'm from Franklin, MA, and I'm expected to graduate in December 2028.

Right now most of my time goes to the Neuroloops Lab at Georgia Tech. I'm the hardware engineer on a wearable that's meant to restore the sense of touch through neuromorphic modulated stimulation. I am responsible for part selection, schematics, and 4-layer flex PCB layout for the whole system. That includes 2 sets of 8-channel EEG/EMG modules and a 90V stimulator that shuts itself off in hardware if anything goes out of range. I'm writing the firmware now, and after that I'm moving the lab's spiking neural network onto an FPGA.

I'm also part of Northeastern's Movement Neuroscience Lab. I started there as a research intern and stayed on as a visiting researcher. Most of my work there is real-time C++ on Xenomai Linux. I've written drivers for TMS and EMG equipment, a platform that adjusts brain stimulation from live EEG while being synchronized to visuomotor force tasks, and an onboard driver for a commercial stimulator. Since all of it runs during experiments with real people, I learned pretty quickly how much timing and safety matter.

What I like most is when the analog side, the digital side, and the firmware all have to work together. That's usually where things break, and figuring out why is the part I enjoy. I want to keep doing that kind of work, whether it's in medical devices or any other hardware product.

Skills

  • Hardware design: schematics, 4-layer and flex PCB layout, analog front ends, isolated power and stimulation circuits
  • Embedded and real-time software: C++, Python, PlatformIO, ESP32, Xenomai real-time Linux
  • Protocols: SPI, I2C, UART, TCP/IP
  • Lab: oscilloscopes, multimeters, power supplies, board bring-up and debugging
