Closed-Loop EEG Neurostimulation Platform

Visiting Researcher · June to Aug. 2026 · C++, real-time Linux, signal processing

The Problem

Most brain stimulation experiments use fixed settings, even though brain activity changes from moment to moment. The lab wanted a system that could watch EEG in real time and adjust stimulation automatically while someone does a motor task.

The Process

I wrote five real-time C++ modules in the RTXI framework, running on Xenomai real-time Linux:

  • A 32-channel EEG collector that streams from the amplifier and pulls out frequency-band features in real time.
  • A stimulation engine that drives 8 configurable channels, including electrical stimulation and TMS triggering.
  • A 6-input force sensor module, a per-trial task generator, and a force display the subject follows.

Then I closed the loop. Stimulation amplitude follows live EEG band power, calibrated to each subject's baseline, with slew-rate limiting so it never jumps suddenly. I also wrote a Python tool that builds, configures, and launches the whole experiment in one step.

The Outcome

Researchers no longer have to adjust amplitude by hand, and the platform opened up new experiments, like having a subject trace the exact stimulation waveform being sent to their own brain. The Python tool turned a long manual setup into something anyone in the lab can run.
