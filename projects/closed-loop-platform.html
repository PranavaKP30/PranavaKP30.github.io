Closed-Loop Stimulation Platform

Visiting Researcher · June to Aug. 2026 · C++, real-time Linux, signal processing

The Problem

Brain stimulation experiments usually use fixed settings, even though the brain's state changes moment to moment. The lab needed a platform that could watch brain activity in real time and adjust stimulation automatically while a participant performed a motor task.

The Process

I built five real-time C++ modules in the RTXI framework, running on Xenomai real-time Linux:

  • A 32-channel EEG collector that streams data from a research amplifier and extracts spectral features in real time.
  • A unified stimulation engine driving 8 configurable channels, including electrical stimulation and TMS triggering.
  • A six-input force collector, a designable per-trial task generator, and a subject-facing force display.

I then closed the loop: stimulation amplitude follows live, baseline-calibrated EEG band power, with slew-rate limiting so changes stay smooth and within safe hardware limits. Finally, I wrote a Python orchestrator that builds, configures, and launches the whole experiment in one step.

The Outcome

The platform replaced manual amplitude adjustment with automatic, real-time control, and it made new experiments possible, such as a participant tracing the exact stimulation waveform being delivered to their own brain. The orchestrator turned a long manual setup into a single reproducible workflow for the whole lab.
