Career goals

Where I'm headed

My long-term goal is to become a hardware engineer who designs products people rely on every day.

The projects I enjoy most combine analog circuits, power electronics, and embedded systems, and they have to work reliably every time. That's true whether the product is a 3D printer, a robot, a wearable, or a medical device, and it's the kind of work I want to build my career around, ideally here in the Boston area.

The roadmap

  • Now: build a strong hardware foundationFinish the Neuroloops wearable, from boards to working firmware, and keep strengthening my PCB design, analog, and embedded skills.
  • 2027: industry experienceComplete a hardware co-op or internship at a Boston company, and learn how products go from prototype to production.
  • 2027 to 2028: deepen my courseworkTake core ECE courses in circuits, electronics, signals, and embedded systems, with electives focused on analog and mixed-signal design, power electronics, and embedded systems.
  • After graduation: hardware engineerJoin a hardware team building real products, and possibly pursue a master's degree to specialize further in analog and mixed-signal design.
  • Long term: lead hardware for products that matterOwn the hardware for a product that makes a real difference in people's lives.

How I'll get there

  • Keep building: every semester, finish at least one real hardware project from schematic to tested board.
  • Get industry experience early: co-ops and internships teach design for manufacturing, testing, and teamwork that research can't.
  • Learn the fundamentals deeply: use my coursework to understand the "why" behind every design choice.
  • Learn from people ahead of me: stay close to mentors in research and industry, and keep asking questions.
