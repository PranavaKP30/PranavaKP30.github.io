Career goals

Where I'm headed

I want to be a hardware engineer working on products people actually depend on, and eventually lead the hardware side of one.

The work I like most mixes analog circuits, power electronics, and embedded systems. Research has taught me a lot about building hardware that's safe to put on a person, but I want to learn how real products get designed, tested, and shipped. I'd like to do that here in the Boston area.

The roadmap

  • 2026: researchStart at Georgia Tech. Design the hardware for the Neuroloops wearable, from schematics and flex PCBs to firmware, and keep working on real-time closed-loop systems at Northeastern.
  • 2027: research + first industry experienceDo a hardware co-op or internship in Boston and see how products go from prototype to production. Keep moving the Neuroloops system forward with testing and the FPGA processor, and get through core circuits, digital design, and electronics classes.
  • 2028: more industry + upper-level classesA second industry role, plus classes in analog electronics, op-amp design, biomedical instrumentation, and DSP. Wrap up my research projects and get them published.
  • 2029: full-time hardware engineerGraduate and join a hardware team full time, working on boards and subsystems for a product that ships.
  • Long term: hardware leadMove into a technical lead role, owning the hardware design for a product and helping newer engineers on the team.

How I'll get there

  • Research: keep owning real systems in the lab, from the first schematic to tested hardware.
  • Industry work: co-ops and internships teach things research doesn't, like design for manufacturing, testing at scale, and working on a team that ships.
  • Coursework: use my classes to learn the theory behind the design decisions I'm already making in the lab.
  • Personal projects: keep building my own stuff, like the EMG module, to try ideas quickly.
ECE roadmap

My course plan

I'm doing the B.S. in EE with two threads, Circuit Technology and Bioengineering. Transfer credit covered my calculus, physics, chemistry, intro computing, and most gen eds, so I could start ECE classes right away and finish in about two and a half years.

Why these threads: the upper-level classes match what I'm already building. Analog Electronics and Op-Amp Design go deeper into the front ends on my EMG and EEG boards. Biomedical Instrumentation and Engineering Electrophysiology cover the signals I'm recording. Signals and Systems and DSP are behind the real-time processing I do in the lab.

If I do a co-op: I'm applying to Spring 2027 hardware co-ops in Boston. If I take one, everything after Fall 2026 moves back one semester and I'd graduate in May 2029 instead of December 2028. I think that's worth it, since real industry experience early on will make the rest of my classes more useful.
