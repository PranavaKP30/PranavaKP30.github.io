Career goals

Where I'm headed

My goal is to become a hardware engineer on products people rely on every day, and eventually lead hardware development for one.

I'm most interested in work that combines analog circuits, digital electronics, and embedded systems. Research has taught me how to build hardware that's safe to use on people. Next, I want to learn how products are designed, tested, and shipped at scale, starting with internships and co-ops in the Boston area.

The roadmap

  • 2026: researchStart at Georgia Tech. Design the hardware for the Neuroloops wearable, from schematics and flex PCBs to firmware, and keep working on real-time closed-loop systems at Northeastern.
  • 2027: industry + researchComplete a spring hardware co-op in Boston to see how products go from prototype to production, followed by summer research in Boston. Continue developing the Neuroloops system by building a neuromorphic processor that runs the spiking neural network on an FPGA, and complete core classes in circuits, digital design, and electronics.
  • 2028: more industry + upper-level classesA second industry role, plus classes in analog electronics, op-amp design, biomedical instrumentation, and DSP. Wrap up my research projects and get them published.
  • 2029: graduate + master'sGraduate with my B.S. in Electrical Engineering and continue into a master's program to go deeper into areas like analog and digital IC design, FPGAs, and hardware for neural interfaces.
  • After my master's: hardware engineerJoin a hardware team full time, designing boards and subsystems for products that ship, ideally on cutting-edge medical or neurotech devices.
  • Long term: hardware leadMove into a technical lead role, owning the hardware design for a product and mentoring newer engineers on the team.

How I'll get there

  • Industry experience: use co-ops and internships to learn what research doesn't teach, including design for manufacturing, testing at scale, and shipping a product as part of a team.
  • Research: continue owning complete hardware systems in the lab, from the first schematic through testing and validation.
  • Personal projects: keep building independent projects like my EMG module to explore new ideas and pick up new skills quickly.
  • Coursework: build a strong foundation in circuits, electronics, and signal processing to understand the theory behind the design decisions I make in practice.
ECE roadmap

My course plan

I'm doing the B.S. in EE with two threads, Circuit Technology and Bioengineering. Transfer credit covered my calculus, physics, chemistry, intro computing, and most gen eds, so I could start ECE classes right away and finish in about two and a half years.

Why these threads: the upper-level classes match what I'm already building. Analog Electronics and Op-Amp Design go deeper into the front ends on my EMG and EEG boards. Biomedical Instrumentation and Engineering Electrophysiology cover the signals I'm recording. Signals and Systems and DSP are behind the real-time processing I do in the lab.

If I do a co-op: I'm applying to Spring 2027 hardware co-ops in Boston. If I take one, everything after Fall 2026 moves back one semester and I'd graduate in May 2029 instead of December 2028. I think that's worth it, since real industry experience early on will make the rest of my classes more useful.
